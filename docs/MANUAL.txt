n001se – MANUAL
Random FM drum sequencer for the M-VAVE FM-1
Firmware 1.5.0 (821)

====================================================================

WHAT IS n001se?

n001se is a custom firmware that turns the M-VAVE FM-1 into a sequencer for random FM percussion, made for live sets. It has five tracks:

  Tracks 1–4: FM drums (Kick, Snare, Hat, Perc, Click, Metal or Clap)
  Track 5:    Acid, a 303-style bass line with its own filter, accent and glide

Each track has its own pattern of 1–8 bars (acid 1–4), and the tracks can have different lengths, so polyrhythms appear by themselves. Patterns are drawn at random within limits you choose, and they can change slowly while they play. Every hit also varies a little in tone, so nothing sounds exactly the same twice.

====================================================================

INSTALLATION

1. Open M-UPGRADE and choose the file n001se-X.Y.Z.fwsc.
2. Follow the steps in M-UPGRADE. A new version must have a higher number than the one already installed.

Over USB the FM-1 shows up as "n001se FM-1", both as a MIDI device and as an audio input (stereo, 44.1 kHz). So you can record n001se straight into your computer over the USB cable.

====================================================================

THE SCREEN

Top:
  The page name, dots showing which page you are on when a button has several pages, LIVE when live play is on, a small dot when there are unsaved changes, and the tempo (BPM, or EXT when MIDI clock sets the tempo). The Setup page also shows the CPU load in %, and "X" with a number if the audio has dropped out.

Middle (the sequencer):
  One row per track, 1–5. The selected row has a grey background.
  On the left: a level meter, the track's name, its length ("2b" = 2 bars, "12st" = 12 steps) and R/D when the track sends to reverb/delay.
  Then 16 steps for one bar. A stronger colour means a higher velocity, and a small mark shows a pitched step. On the acid track accents are brighter, and slides show as a bridge to the next step.

Below the tracks:
  The bar numbers 1–8. The bar shown on the keys is white, and underlined if you have locked it.
  The phrase row: eight bars, the eighth red. During the eighth bar the beats count 1–4 on the far right.

Big numbers:
  In bars 4 and 8 of the phrase the beats 1–4 are shown big and see-through over the sequencer. Each number fades until the next one comes, and in bar 8 they are red. It makes it easier to find the right spot for breaks.

The parameter box:
  When you turn a knob, the parameter's name and value show big over the sequencer. The box fades away shortly after you stop turning.

Bottom:
  The parameters of the four knobs on the page you are on. The value of the knob you turn turns orange.

BUTTONS

PLAY       Start/stop.
REC        New patterns on all tracks.
SEQ        Switches between SEQ (step editing) and LIVE (play the sounds on the keys).
SAVE       Opens the preset list to save (see PRESETS).
           Hold SAVE for 1.5 s: asks "RESET?". Press SAVE again within 5 s to reset everything to the defaults (the MIDI channel is kept). Anything else keeps everything.
ARP        Opens the preset list to load. Press ARP again to load the selected preset.
OCT- / OCT+  Selects the previous/next track. In the preset list: moves one slot.
SEL        Scenes. What SEL does is chosen on the Scene page (GLOBAL, page 5), SEL mode:
           Chance: a scene by chance. Which tracks play (at least two) and how much effect each sound gets (reverb, delay, filter, drive and Mod) are drawn at random. Press SEL again and everything is back as it was before, even if you turned things meanwhile. While the scene plays, the mute keys (black 5–9) set the original's mutes, not the scene's: you choose what will play when you come back. Their lights show the original's mutes meanwhile.
           A/B: two scenes to switch between. The first press makes a copy of everything (patterns, sounds, effects, settings) and you go on in the copy, B: change anything, make new rhythms, new sounds. Press SEL and you are back in A, just as you left it. Press again and you are in B where you last were. So you can work up two scenes and switch between them.
           Copy: a copy to play with that is never kept. SEL makes a copy of everything and you change what you like. SEL again takes you back to the original, and the changes are gone. The next SEL makes a new copy.
           SEL is lit while you are in the scene, in B or in the copy. The Setup and Scene pages are never swapped. Changing SEL mode, loading a preset or a reset forgets the copy. Swapping patterns or sounds gives a very short break in the sound.

Page buttons (another press goes to the button's next page):
HOME       Pattern
EDIT       Track
ENV        Sound → Tone
FX         Track FX → Mod → FX bus
LFO        Drift
GLOBAL     Master → Glue → Groove → Setup → Scene

ENCODERS

SELECT     Selects the track. In the preset list: selects the slot.
ALGO       Steps through all pages.
PRESETS    A quick knob for any parameter. Glue by default. You choose what it controls on the Setup page (the "Presets" knob): a global parameter, a parameter of the selected drum track or a 303 parameter, for example "303 Cutoff". In the preset list it steps through the slots.
MASTER     Main volume.
K1–K4      The four knobs under the screen. What they do depends on the page.

THE KEYS

WHITE KEYS (16 = the 16 steps of the bar shown)

In SEQ mode:
  Press an empty step: the step turns on.
  Press and release a step that is on: the step turns off.
  Hold a step and turn:
    Drum tracks: K1 = velocity, K2 = pitch (−24 to +24)
    The acid track: K1 = note (−12 to +24), K2 = accent off/on, K3 = slide off/on (right = on, left = off). A slide belongs to the note that slides on: it glides into the next step's note.
  A step you edited while holding it stays when you let go.

In LIVE mode:
  The keys play the sounds directly, four keys per drum sound (tracks 1–4), up the scale.

In the preset list:
  Pick a slot directly among the 16 slots on the list's page.

BLACK KEYS (11, from the left)

  1   Previous bar (show and edit)
  2   Next bar
      1 + 2 together: follow the playback again
  3   UNDO
  4   REDO
  5   Mute track 1
  6   Mute track 2
  7   Mute track 3
  8   Mute track 4
  9   Mute track 5 (acid)
      Turning a track back on also selects it for editing.
  10  New pattern for the selected track
  11  New sound for the selected track

UNDO/REDO:
  Up to 12 steps back. One knob turn counts as one step, and so does a held step with what you change while holding it. Undo works on settings, patterns, sounds, mutes and preset loads. Settings are undone without a break in the sound. Undoing a pattern or a sound gives a very short break.

THE PAGES

The drum tracks (tracks 1–4):

PATTERN (HOME)
  Bars       The pattern's length, 1–8 bars
  Hits min   The fewest hits in the pattern
  Hits max   The most hits in the pattern (equal to min = exactly that many)
  Evolve     Chance in % per loop that one step of the pattern changes

TRACK (EDIT)
  Rnd len    Random length: Fixed, or 1–N bars. Sometimes the last bar is shorter, for odd lengths.
  Pitch      How much the steps may vary in pitch (+-N semitones), Root = no variation
  Level      The track's volume
  Mute       Off/on

SOUND (ENV, page 1)
  Deviance   How much the tone varies from hit to hit
  Length     The sound's length
  Edge       Attack and bite
  Ring       Resonance and ring

TONE (ENV, page 2)
  Kind       The kind of sound: Kick, Snare, Hat, Perc, Click, Metal, Clap. A clap is three or four quick bursts of noise and then its tail, a little different on every hit. Takes effect with the next new sound (black 11).
  Ring tone  The resonance's pitch
  Tune       Tuning, −24 to +24 semitones

TRACK FX (FX, page 1)
  Reverb     Send to the reverb. The amount is random per hit up to the level you set, and sometimes nothing is sent at all.
  Delay      Send to the delay, the same way
  Filter     How often the moving filter is on the track
  Drive      Warm saturation with even harmonics, compensated so the volume does not rise

MOD (FX, page 2)
  A modulation effect per sound. It is not global: each track has its own.
  Mod        The effect: Off, Phaser, Flanger, Chorus, Ringmod or Tremolo
  Amount     How much. Phaser: the sweep's width and feedback. Flanger: the sweep's depth and feedback. Chorus: depth and amount. Ringmod: amount. Tremolo: depth.
  Rate       Speed. The sweep runs at 0.05–8 Hz (tremolo 0.5–32 Hz). For Ringmod, Rate is the carrier's frequency, 30–1920 Hz: low gives a shaky tremolo, high a metallic ring.
  Chance     The share of hits that get the effect, 0–100 %. Drawn for every hit, just like the sends.
  The sweep runs all the time, also between hits, so every hit lands at a new place in the sweep. The volume is compensated, so the effect changes the tone, not the loudness.

The acid track (track 5) has its own parameters on the same pages:

PATTERN    Bars (1–4), Notes (how many steps have notes), Slides, Evolve
TRACK      Accents, Octaves (chance of octave jumps), Scale (Minor, Phrygian, Dorian, Penta, Blues, Harm min), Level
SOUND      Cutoff, Reso, Env mod, Decay
TONE       Wave (Saw, Square, Pulse, Tri, Sine, Duo), Accent (the accent's strength), Tune, Chaos (how far new patterns leave the rules, 0–100 %)
TRACK FX   Reverb, Delay, Filter, Drive
MOD        Mod, Amount, Rate, Chance (as on the drum tracks, but Chance is drawn per note, and a slid note keeps the previous note's choice)

How the acid line plays, as a TB-303:
  Gate       A note sounds for half a step. A slide holds it into the next step, and the next note glides there without a new attack: the volume and the filter envelope go on. A note that is not slid into starts again. A rest closes the gate, so a slide into a rest makes a longer note. Slides in a row make one long movement, also from the last step into the first.
  Slide      The pitch glides at the same speed whatever the interval (an RC of 45 ms), so a slide of one or two octaves may not get there before the next step; the next note goes on from where the pitch is.
  Filter     A model of the 303's: a diode ladder (about 18 dB/oct in the octave above the cutoff) with a high-pass in the resonance feedback, so the bass stays when the resonance goes up. At high resonance it sings and saturates, but never runs away.
  Accent     Louder and brighter. The brightness goes through a sweep: accents in a row build up, the more the higher the resonance.

How a new acid pattern is drawn (Black 10, E1, REC):
  1. The rhythm: likeliest on the one, then the offbeat eighths and the beats, with rests between. It is a motif of a bar (or half a bar twice) that repeats over the bars with small changes, most at the end of bars 2 and 4.
  2. The notes: the root and the fifth most, then the third and the seventh; mostly small steps or the same note, now and then a leap, now and then a note outside the scale.
  3. The octaves (Octaves), the 303 way: notes side by side are pushed apart. Next to a low note the next goes up an octave, after one up the next comes back down (now and then below the root). Up likelier off the beat. At 15 % about four in ten neighbours are an octave apart, at 35 % and up most of them.
  4. The accents (Accents): where they push the rhythm, off the beat and after a rest.
  5. The slides (Slides): where they move the line, most into another note and most of all into an octave jump.
  Chaos moves every one of these chances towards pure chance: at 0 % the rules, at 50 % half way, at 100 % any note, octaves up to two, accents and slides anywhere, and no motif. Evolve follows the same rules.

Global pages:

FX BUS (FX, page 3)
  Reverb     The reverb's return level (0 = off)
  Delay      The delay's return level (0 = off)
  Filter     Scales every track's filter (0 = off)
  Drive      Scales every track's drive (0 = off)

DRIFT (LFO)
  Chaos      More randomness everywhere, and glitches that come more often the higher Chaos is:
             Beat repeat: the last 1/32, 1/16 or 1/8 repeats, for an eighth at most, then an eighth goes by before the next glitch (a repeat, a pause, a repeat). With swing it repeats on the swung grid.
             Reverse: the last stretch plays backwards.
             Tape stop: the mix slows down to a stop like a tape machine, and then you are back.
             Where the glitches come:
             In bars 4 and 8 of the phrase they come on the beats, and mostly in the last quarter, as a way into the next bar. They never run past the end of the bar. At Chaos 40 about one in three of those bars gets a glitch, at Chaos 100 nearly all of them.
             In the other bars they come only now and then, always on an eighth between the beats (never on the quarters), and they are only an eighth long.
  Motion     How fast the effects glide and move over time
  Mutade     In bars 4 and 8 of the phrase a different set of tracks plays by chance, a quarter at a time: muted tracks may come in and playing ones drop out. It happens most often in the last quarter. At the next quarter it is back to your mutes (or a new set), and after the bar it is always back. 0 = off (the default).
  Space      The reverb and the delay move between places of their own. The reverb: a small room, a room, the usual one, a larger room and a big hall (length and tone). The delay: a dotted eighth (the usual one), an eighth, a sixteenth and a quarter, still ping-pong. 0 = still, as before (the default). The higher, the more often they move, but always slowly: a glide takes 1 to 4 bars, and a place is held at least 4 bars (at 100) or 10 (at 50). At 100 they move about every 8 bars. The delay glides its time like an analog delay, so the repeats bend in pitch on the way (up when it gets shorter, down when it gets longer), more the shorter the glide. Back at 0, both go home over 2 bars.
             With Space up, SEL also moves them, by the same chance as Space (at 100 every time), over one bar.

MASTER (GLOBAL, page 1)
  Hi cut     Low-pass filter on the whole mix (100 = Open)
  Lo cut     High-pass filter on the whole mix (0 = Open)
  Reso       The filters' resonance
  Glue       A light mastering chain on the whole mix, its amount (how hard it works). 0 = off (the whole chain), 100 = a little over the top with light saturation. The loudness is compensated, so Glue makes the mix tighter and more finished but not louder: the volume is Level's. After it there is always a limiter, at −1 dBFS with the Glue on (−0.3 dBFS off).

GLUE (GLOBAL, page 2): how the Glue sounds. Its chain: compressor → EQ → width → inflator → limiter.
  Glue mix   Parallel compression: how much of the compressed mix against what goes in. 100 = all compressed, 50 = half (the default: tight, but the hits keep their punch). The compressor has a gentle transient expander in it, so hits come through.
  Glue EQ    A mastering EQ that listens: it follows the balance between lows and highs and leans the mix towards a pink-noise slope of −4.5 dB per octave, adds a little foundation under 90 Hz and air over 9 kHz, takes a little box (90–700 Hz) away, and turns 2–9 kHz down while it is harsh (de-harsh). 50 = the set it was tuned to (the default), 100 = twice as much, 0 = flat.
  Color      An inflator (after Oxford's curve): denser and fuller near the top without clipping. 0 = off, 100 = all of it. Default 50.
  Width      The stereo width: 0 = mono, 50 = as it is, 100 = twice as wide. The sides under 120 Hz are always taken out (the lows in mono), so the kick and the bass stay centred. Default 60.

GROOVE (GLOBAL, page 3)
  Tempo      60–200 BPM (stays put while MIDI clock is in control)
  Swing      0–100
  Loose      Human unevenness in timing and strength
  Max hits   How many drum tracks may hit on the same step, 1–4 (track 1 always plays). Default: 4

SETUP (GLOBAL, page 4)
  Level      Overall level
  MIDI ch    All or 1–16
  Presets    What the PRESETS encoder controls
  USB audio  On/Off. Off sends silence over USB and saves CPU.
  (The CPU load shows at the top right of this page.)

SCENE (GLOBAL, page 5)
  SEL mode   Chance, A/B or Copy: what SEL does (see SEL under BUTTONS). Default: Chance

====================================================================

PRESETS

64 slots.

Save:
  1. Press SAVE to open the list.
  2. Pick a slot with SELECT, the PRESETS encoder, OCT-/OCT+ or a white key.
  3. Press SAVE again to save.

Load:
  1. Press ARP to open the list.
  2. Pick a slot the same way as above.
  3. Press ARP again to load.

Any other button closes the list without doing anything. Loading an empty slot keeps what you have.

A preset saves everything: patterns, sounds, the acid line and all settings.

Note that saving mutes the sound for a moment, because the flash memory is being written.

AUTOSAVE:
  The project saves itself 4 seconds after the last change, but only when playback is stopped and it is quiet. It never saves in the middle of a live set. When you switch the FM-1 on, it starts where you were.

MIDI clock:

LIGHTS

PLAY is lit while playing, and SEQ is lit in SEQ mode.

White keys:
  In SEQ mode they show the steps of the bar. The playback shows as a faint light walking over the keys.
  In LIVE mode they light up with their sound.

Black keys:
  1/2      Faintly lit in the direction you can go. Blink when the bar is locked.
  3/4      Faintly lit when there is something to undo or redo.
  5–9      Faint = the track plays, bright = a hit right now, off = muted.

The other buttons are faintly lit so you can read them in the dark.

TIPS FOR LIVE SETS

- Give the tracks different lengths (Bars or Rnd len), so they drift against each other and make polyrhythms.
- Evolve at 5–15 keeps the patterns alive without losing their character.
- Put an important parameter on the PRESETS encoder, for example 303 Cutoff, Glue or Chaos, so you always have it at hand whatever the page.
- Use the big beat numbers in bars 4 and 8 to place breaks and mutes in the right spot.
- Put Mod on the hi-hat with Chance around 30, so some hits get a sweeping flanger or phaser while the rest stay dry.
- Mutade at 30–50 builds tension before every round without touching the mutes yourself.
- Press SEL for a new scene in the middle of a set, and SEL again to land back. With SEL mode A/B you can build a second version of the whole set and switch to it for a big change.
- Pulled up a new pattern you did not want? Press UNDO (black 3).
